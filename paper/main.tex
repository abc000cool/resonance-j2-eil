\title{Estimation-in-the-loop J2 formation-keeping: a Pareto
characterization of relative-navigation accuracy versus delta-V cost}
\author{Ansh Pathak\thanks{Independent researcher; pathakansh10@gmail.com}}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Formation-flying spacecraft in low Earth orbit must actively suppress the
differential drift induced by the $J_2$ zonal harmonic, and the delta-V cost
of doing so depends jointly on the controller, the relative-motion model
used for prediction, and the accuracy of the on-board relative navigation
filter. Prior systematic treatments, most notably Koenig \&
D'Amico~\cite{koenig2018tradeoffs}, characterize this trade in a linear
time-invariant, LQR-only setting with the estimator abstracted as a static
covariance floor. We extend that analysis to a controller- and
plant-conditional characterization with \emph{actual filters running in the
loop}: three controllers (LQR, receding-horizon MPC, closed-form impulsive
Chernick--D'Amico), three relative-motion state-transition matrices
(Schweighart--Sedwick, Gim--Alfriend, Koenig--Guffanti--D'Amico), and three
measurement architectures (carrier-phase differential GPS, inter-satellite
RF ranging, angles-only optical) coupled through a Joseph-form error-state
EKF and driven against a numerical $J_2$--$J_4$ truth cross-validated
against Orekit to millimeter relative agreement. A two-tier Monte-Carlo
campaign (12{,}960 screening trials; 32{,}900 numerical-truth trials)
yields three principal results. First, a reliability map: closed-loop
divergence is common at realistic sensor accuracies, affecting 16--22\% of
trials depending on the controller, concentrating in angles-only
navigation, and rendering some controller--sensor pairings infeasible
outright. Second, a filter-consistency result with methodological
consequences. Process noise calibrated the standard way, against open-loop
propagation residuals, leaves the filter overconfident by more than three
orders of magnitude in normalized innovation squared, which \emph{inverts}
the apparent delta-V-versus-accuracy trade. The effect is worst for the
most accurate dynamics model, and consistency restored by closed-loop
covariance matching recovers the physically expected trade at the cost of
noise feed-through. The residual error is secular, so no white-noise
process model can represent it faithfully, which is a quantitative argument
for bias-augmented (Schmidt--Kalman) formulations in formation-keeping
filters. Third, with a consistent filter the navigation-accuracy ``knee''
becomes measurable: for LQR under CDGPS it sits at the 3--10~cm level, so
centimetre-class relative GPS saturates the fuel benefit, and controllers
respond to degraded navigation differently, with LQR paying in fuel while
MPC holds fuel constant and pays in accuracy. All software, data, and
figures are openly released and exactly reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
All software, campaign data, and figure-generation scripts are openly
available.
The development repository is at
\url{https://github.com/abc000cool/resonance-j2-eil}. The archive of record,
containing the source, the full 45{,}860-trial campaign as per-grid-point
Parquet files, and this PDF, is deposited at
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21696853}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.21696853},
a version-independent identifier that always resolves to the newest release
(v0.1.1 accompanies this manuscript).
Every figure regenerates from the archived data with
\texttt{python scripts/make\_all.py --phase figures} (equivalently
\texttt{snakemake -c8 figures}), the knee table with
\texttt{scripts/find\_knees.py}, and the cross-validation table with
\texttt{scripts/crossvalidate\_orekit.py}; every Monte-Carlo trial is
reproducible in isolation from its recorded seed. An interactive summary of the
study is at \url{https://when-the-filter-lies.vercel.app}.
