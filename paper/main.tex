\title{Estimation-in-the-loop J2 formation-keeping: a Pareto
characterization of relative-navigation accuracy versus delta-V cost}
\author{Ansh Pathak\thanks{Independent researcher; pathakansh10@gmail.com}}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Formation-flying spacecraft in low Earth orbit must actively suppress the
differential drift induced by the $J_2$ zonal harmonic, and the delta-V cost
of doing so depends jointly on the controller, the relative-motion model
used for prediction, and the accuracy of the on-board relative navigation
filter. Prior systematic treatments --- most notably Koenig \&
D'Amico~\cite{koenig2018tradeoffs} --- characterize this trade in a linear
time-invariant, LQR-only setting with the estimator abstracted as a static
covariance floor. We extend that analysis to a controller- and
plant-conditional characterization with \emph{actual filters running in the
loop}: three controllers (LQR, receding-horizon MPC, closed-form impulsive
Chernick--D'Amico), three relative-motion state-transition matrices
(Schweighart--Sedwick, Gim--Alfriend, Koenig--Guffanti--D'Amico), and three
measurement architectures (carrier-phase differential GPS, inter-satellite
RF ranging, angles-only optical) coupled through a Joseph-form error-state
EKF and driven against a numerical $J_2$--$J_4$ truth cross-validated
against Orekit to millimeter relative agreement. A two-tier Monte-Carlo
campaign (12{,}960 screening trials; 32{,}900 numerical-truth trials)
yields three principal results. First, a reliability map: closed-loop
divergence is common at realistic sensor accuracies (16--22\% of all
trials), concentrates in angles-only navigation, and renders some
controller--sensor pairings infeasible outright. Second, a
filter-consistency result with methodological consequences: process noise
calibrated the standard way --- against open-loop propagation residuals ---
leaves the filter overconfident by up to four orders of magnitude in
normalized innovation squared, \emph{inverting} the apparent
delta-V-versus-accuracy trade; the effect is worst for the most accurate
dynamics model, and consistency restored by closed-loop covariance matching
recovers the physically expected trade at the cost of noise feed-through.
The residual error is secular, so no white-noise process model can
represent it faithfully --- a quantitative argument for bias-augmented
(Schmidt--Kalman) formulations in formation-keeping filters. Third, with a
consistent filter the navigation-accuracy ``knee'' becomes measurable: for
LQR under CDGPS it sits at the 3--10~cm level, and controllers respond to
degraded navigation differently --- LQR pays in fuel, MPC holds fuel
constant and pays in accuracy. All software, data, and figures are openly
released and exactly reproducible.
\end{abstract}
