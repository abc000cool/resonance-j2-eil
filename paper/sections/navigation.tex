\section{Relative navigation and error-state filtering}\label{sec:nav}

\subsection{Measurement architectures}

Three measurement architectures spanning the accuracy and cost range of
flown relative-navigation systems are modeled (Table~\ref{tab:sensors}).
\emph{Carrier-phase differential GPS} (CDGPS) provides three-dimensional
relative position in the LVLH frame; flight systems have demonstrated
millimeter-to-centimeter accuracy on PRISMA, TanDEM-X, and CanX-4/-5
\cite{busse2003cdgps,kroes2006thesis,kroes2005precise,
montenbruck2011tandemxnav,kahr2018canx}. \emph{Inter-satellite RF ranging}
provides range and range-rate along the line of sight, the architecture
flown on MMS \cite{roscoe2011mms}. \emph{Angles-only optical navigation}
provides two bearing angles and no range. Its weak observability, the
classical range-ambiguity dilemma formalized by Woffinden and Geller
\cite{woffinden2009observability}, is broken in practice by $J_2$ and by
maneuvers \cite{damico2013argon,gaias2014anglesonly,sullivan2021nonlinear,
willis2024polynomial}, both of which are present in the closed-loop
scenarios studied here. Each architecture is swept over roughly two decades
of $1\sigma$ accuracy bracketing its flown performance. Measurements are
generated from the true relative state with additive white Gaussian noise
at every filter step ($\Delta t = 60$~s).

\subsection{Error-state extended Kalman filter}

Navigation is performed by an error-state EKF whose prediction model is one
of the three $J_2$-bearing analytic STMs of Section~\ref{sec:models}
(S--S in LVLH coordinates; GA and KGD in their native element-difference
coordinates, with the ROE$\to$LVLH Jacobian composing the measurement
model). The covariance update uses the Joseph form for numerical symmetry,
and control accelerations and impulses are fed through to the state
prediction via the plant's discrete control-input matrix. Measurement
Jacobians for the three architectures are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:jacobians}, and the filter equations in
Appendix~\ref{app:filters}. Filters are initialized from the true state
perturbed consistently with the initial covariance, and the controller is
inhibited for a two-orbit warm-up while the filter converges. An unscented
(sigma-point) variant is implemented and verified for use as an ablation;
all campaign results below use the EKF.

\subsection{Process-noise calibration}

The filter's process noise is a white-acceleration power spectral density
$q$, mapped to the native state coordinates per step. Its value is
calibrated per dynamics model in the conventional way: propagate the truth,
collect one-step \emph{open-loop} prediction residuals of each STM over
three days, and fit $q$ to the residual velocity variance
(\texttt{scripts/calibrate\_q.py}). This yields
$q \approx 5.5\times10^{-10}$, $8.3\times10^{-13}$, and
$9.4\times10^{-15}\,(\mathrm{m/s^2})^2\,\mathrm{s}$ for S--S, GA, and KGD
respectively, carried in the campaign configuration as
$5.5\times10^{-10}$, $8.0\times10^{-13}$, and $1.0\times10^{-14}$. These
values span nearly five orders of magnitude, tracking the fidelity ordering
of Fig.~\ref{fig:validation}, so the calibration hands the most accurate
model the smallest process noise.

Filter statistical consistency is monitored by the standard normalized
innovation squared (NIS, expectation $\dim z$) and normalized estimation
error squared (NEES, expectation $\dim x$), both verified on linear
systems in the test suite. Section~\ref{sec:results} shows that the
open-loop calibration above, despite being carried out exactly as intended,
produces filters that are severely inconsistent in the closed loop, and
that this single implementation choice, easily hidden inside any
estimation-in-the-loop study, qualitatively inverts the headline trade
curve.
