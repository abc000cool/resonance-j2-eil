\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper set out to extend the navigation-versus-control trade analysis
of Koenig and D'Amico \cite{koenig2018tradeoffs} from a covariance-floor
abstraction to a campaign with real filters in the loop --- three
controllers, three relative-motion STMs, and three measurement
architectures against a numerical $J_2$--$J_4$ truth, 45{,}860 trials in
all. The extension delivered the intended products: a validated two-tier
campaign methodology (screening-to-full rank correlation $\rho = 0.83$), a
controller--sensor reliability map with divergence probabilities and
explicitly marked infeasible pairings, perfect-navigation Pareto baselines,
and threshold-robust navigation-accuracy knees --- for LQR under CDGPS,
3--10~cm, beyond which better sensors buy no fuel.

The study's central finding, however, is the one it did not set out to
produce. Running the estimator explicitly exposed a failure chain that the
covariance-floor abstraction structurally cannot see: process noise
calibrated against open-loop propagation residuals leaves the closed-loop
filter overconfident by more than three orders of magnitude in NIS, worst
for the most accurate dynamics model, and the resulting trade curves are not
merely noisy but \emph{inverted} --- fuel cost appearing to fall as sensors
degrade. Restoring consistency by closed-loop covariance matching recovers
the physically expected trades and reveals a clean controller distinction
(LQR pays for poor navigation in fuel, MPC in accuracy), at a quantified
performance cost; and the residual NEES optimism demonstrates that no
white-noise process model can faithfully represent a secular model error.
The constructive implication is a bias-augmented (Schmidt--Kalman) filter
formulation, which this framework is positioned to evaluate next, alongside
the configured certainty-equivalence stress test.

Every figure in this paper regenerates from the released code and data with
one command, the tables from two named scripts, and the campaign is seeded
for exact reproducibility down to the individual trial. We hope the framework serves
both as a design tool --- reading delta-V consequences off a navigation
budget --- and as a cautionary, fully inspectable example of how easily an
estimation-in-the-loop study can be silently corrupted by its filter
tuning.
