\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Formation-flying missions in low Earth orbit (LEO) --- PRISMA
\cite{damico2012prisma}, TanDEM-X \cite{montenbruck2008tandemx}, GRACE-FO
\cite{kornfeld2019gracefo}, NASA Starling \cite{kruger2025starling}, and the
upcoming VISORS telescope \cite{guffanti2023visors} --- must continuously
counteract the differential secular drift induced by the Earth-oblateness
$J_2$ harmonic. The controller that performs this formation-keeping never
sees the true relative state: it acts on the estimate produced by a relative
navigation filter, so the achievable delta-V budget is a joint function of
the navigation accuracy, the controller family, and the fidelity of the
relative-motion model used for prediction.

The most directly comparable systematic treatment of this coupling,
Koenig \& D'Amico \cite{koenig2018tradeoffs}, characterizes the trade in an
LTI,
near-circular, LQR-only setting with the estimator abstracted as a static
covariance floor. This paper extends that analysis along five axes:
(i) three controllers instead of one; (ii) three relative-motion STMs
including the modern quasi-nonsingular ROE STM
\cite{koenig2017stm}; (iii) three realistic measurement architectures with
actual filters running in the loop, not a static covariance; (iv) a Pareto
\emph{surface} instead of a single trade curve; and (v) divergence
statistics that locate where certainty-equivalent control fails outright,
instead of assuming throughout that it holds. The same questions have
recently been taken up for cislunar
formations, where long-duration station-keeping strategies
\cite{foss2025stationkeeping} and impulsive relative-motion control via
reachable-set theory \cite{hunter2025cislunar} are being developed against
far less certain dynamics; the LEO setting studied here has the advantage
that the dominant perturbation is precisely known, so any closed-loop
pathology that appears must be attributed to the estimation-and-control
architecture rather than to dynamical ignorance.

Running the estimator explicitly, rather than abstracting it, turned out to
matter more than anticipated. When the extended Kalman filter's process
noise is calibrated the way it usually is in practice --- by fitting a
white-acceleration power spectral density to short-horizon, \emph{open-loop}
propagation residuals --- the resulting filter is statistically
inconsistent in the 30-day \emph{closed-loop} regime: overconfident by more
than three orders of magnitude in normalized innovation squared (NIS) and,
contrary to intuition, worst for the \emph{most accurate} dynamics model,
which the calibration rewards with the smallest process noise. The
practical consequence is not subtle: the apparent
delta-V-versus-navigation-accuracy trade \emph{inverts} --- fuel cost
appears to fall as sensors degrade --- and a mission designer reading such
a curve would draw exactly the wrong conclusion. We diagnose the mechanism,
show by direct experiment that it is the secular error of the analytic
state-transition matrices rather than control feed-through, restore
consistency by closed-loop covariance matching, and quantify what
consistency costs in closed-loop performance.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an open-source, exactly reproducible estimation-in-the-loop
    simulation framework coupling three controllers, three relative-motion
    STMs, three measurement architectures, and a numerical $J_2$--$J_4$
    truth cross-validated against Orekit to millimeter-level relative
    agreement (Sections~\ref{sec:models}--\ref{sec:method});
  \item a two-tier Monte-Carlo campaign --- an STM-surrogate screening tier
    (12{,}960 trials) whose per-cell delta-V medians rank-correlate at
    $\rho = 0.83$ with the numerical-truth full tier (32{,}900 trials),
    validating surrogate screening as a design practice
    (Section~\ref{sec:method});
  \item a controller--sensor \emph{reliability map}: divergence
    probabilities and outright-infeasible pairings at realistic sensor
    accuracies --- information that point-estimate trade studies do not
    surface (Section~\ref{sec:results});
  \item the filter-consistency result summarized above, including the
    demonstration that a white-noise process model cannot represent the
    secular STM error, and the quantitative case for bias-augmented
    (Schmidt--Kalman) formulations in formation-keeping filters
    (Section~\ref{sec:results});
  \item navigation-accuracy knees measured with a threshold-robust metric
    under a consistent filter, and the observation that controllers degrade
    along different axes --- LQR pays for poor navigation in fuel, MPC in
    accuracy (Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:models} summarizes the
relative-motion models and formation geometries and validates them against
the numerical truth; Section~\ref{sec:nav} describes the measurement
architectures and the error-state filter; Section~\ref{sec:controllers}
the three controllers. Section~\ref{sec:method} covers the truth
propagator and its cross-validation, the campaign design, and the robust
knee metric. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents results in six blocks,
from uncontrolled drift through the consistency analysis to the corrected
trade curves. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses actionability,
limitations, and follow-on work; Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
