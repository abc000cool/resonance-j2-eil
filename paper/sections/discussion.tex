\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{For mission designers.}
The corrected results give design guidance of the form the introduction
promised. For a Starling-class swarm at kilometer separation
\cite{kruger2025starling}, centimeter-class CDGPS saturates the LQR fuel
benefit, since the knee sits at 3--10~cm, so investing in millimeter-class
relative GPS buys no delta-V. A VISORS-class precision formation
\cite{guffanti2023visors} is instead bounded by the controller's accuracy
floor rather than by the fuel knee (Fig.~\ref{fig:perfect}). The controller
choice determines which currency degrades with navigation: LQR holds
accuracy and spends fuel, MPC holds fuel and spends accuracy, and the
closed-form impulsive scheme is the most failure-resistant of the three
across the reliability map as well as the cheapest, at the cost of a
$\sim$45~m error floor. The reliability map is itself a design input, since
angles-only navigation at these formation scales frequently cannot close the
loop at all with certainty-equivalent controllers, so an angles-only mission
accepts a qualitatively different risk posture, beyond any loss of accuracy
\cite{damico2013argon,gaias2014anglesonly}.

\paragraph{For filter designers.}
The consistency result generalizes beyond this simulation. The failure chain
involves no exotic ingredient: calibrate $Q$ against short-horizon open-loop
residuals, deploy in a long-horizon closed loop, and obtain an overconfident
filter whose pathology is worst for the best dynamics model. Any
estimation-in-the-loop trade study, and any flight filter tuned on
propagation residuals, can walk into it. Three practical mitigations follow
from the analysis. First, monitor NIS in the loop and not only in open-loop
tuning, since the inversion signature of cost falling as sensors degrade is
diagnostic. Second, if white-noise $Q$ must be used, match it in closed
loop, but expect to pay in noise feed-through and expect NEES to remain
optimistic in the unobserved subspace, because a white process model cannot
represent a secular error. Third, the principled fix is to give the secular
residual a state, through a Schmidt--Kalman or consider-covariance
formulation, a bias-augmented filter, or an empirically fitted higher-order
prediction model of the kind proposed by Willis and D'Amico
\cite{willis2024polynomial}. Quantifying how much of the consistency gap a
one-per-axis bias state closes is the clearest follow-on experiment this
framework enables.

\paragraph{Relation to uncertainty-aware control.}
Certainty equivalence is the baseline throughout, and its breakdown is
visible both in the divergence statistics and in the noise feed-through cost
of the consistent filter. The natural next step is control that consumes the
covariance rather than ignoring it. The covariance-aware LQR variant
implemented here, which inflates the state weight in the spirit of cautious
control, is the minimal version, while covariance steering
\cite{ridderhof2021covariance} and reachable-set-constrained impulsive
planning \cite{chernick2021reachable,hunter2025cislunar} are the mature
formulations. The framework's configured certainty-equivalence stress test,
covariance-aware LQR at the loose-navigation corner in
\texttt{config/ablation.yaml}, is deferred to follow-on work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The truth is $J_2$--$J_4$ zonal-only by design. Differential drag and solar
radiation pressure, which are significant for low-altitude or
high-area-ratio formations \cite{gaias2015drag,guffanti2019srp}, are
excluded so that model error is attributable, and including them would
enlarge the secular residual the consistency analysis identifies rather than
remove it. Actuator limits are applied asymmetrically: the LQR acceleration
is magnitude-limited at $2\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, whereas the MPC
and impulsive schemes are run without a delta-V bound, so their cost figures
are lower bounds with respect to a thrust-limited vehicle and the three-way
cost comparison should be read with that in mind. The campaign also fixes a
near-circular chief at $i = 98^\circ$, a single 1-km formation scale, and
one tuning per controller; the perfect-state sweeps vary tightness but the
estimation-in-the-loop grid does not. Measurement models are white and
unbiased, with no multipath, integer-ambiguity, or attitude-coupling errors,
so the reliability map is if anything optimistic. The main results use the
EKF, while the implemented UKF ablation and the covariance-aware LQR stress
test are configured but not part of the reported campaign. Finally, the
analytic models are first-order in $J_2$; at 1-km scale the linearization
error is subdominant to the secular $J_2$ residual, but both grow with
formation size.
