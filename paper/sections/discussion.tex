\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{For mission designers.}
Read directly, the corrected results give design guidance of the form the
introduction promised. For a Starling-class swarm at kilometer separation
\cite{kruger2025starling}, centimeter-class CDGPS saturates the LQR fuel
benefit --- the knee sits at 3--10~cm, so investing in millimeter-class
relative GPS buys no delta-V --- while a VISORS-class precision formation
\cite{guffanti2023visors} is bounded not by the fuel knee but by the
controller's accuracy floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:perfect}). The controller choice
determines \emph{which currency} degrades with navigation: LQR holds
accuracy and spends fuel; MPC holds fuel and spends accuracy; the
closed-form impulsive scheme is the most failure-resistant of the three
across the reliability map and the cheapest, at the cost of a
$\sim$45~m error floor. And the reliability map itself is a design input:
angles-only navigation at these formation scales frequently cannot close
the loop at all with certainty-equivalent controllers, so an angles-only
mission buys not just worse accuracy but a qualitatively different risk
posture \cite{damico2013argon,gaias2014anglesonly}.

\paragraph{For filter designers.}
The consistency result generalizes beyond this simulation. The failure
chain --- calibrate $Q$ against short-horizon open-loop residuals, deploy
in a long-horizon closed loop, obtain an overconfident filter whose
pathology is \emph{worst for the best dynamics model} --- involves no
exotic ingredient; any estimation-in-the-loop trade study, and any flight
filter tuned on propagation residuals, can walk into it. Three practical
mitigations follow from the analysis. (1) Monitor NIS in the loop, not
only in open-loop tuning; the inversion signature (cost falling as sensors
degrade) is diagnostic. (2) If white-noise $Q$ must be used, match it in
closed loop; but expect to pay in noise feed-through, and expect NEES to
remain optimistic in the unobserved subspace --- a white process model
cannot represent a secular error. (3) The principled fix is to give the
secular residual a state: a Schmidt--Kalman / consider-covariance or
bias-augmented formulation, or an empirically fitted higher-order
prediction model of the kind proposed by Willis and D'Amico
\cite{willis2024polynomial}. Quantifying how much of the consistency gap a
one-per-axis bias state closes is the clearest follow-on experiment this
framework enables.

\paragraph{Relation to uncertainty-aware control.}
Certainty equivalence is the baseline throughout, and its breakdown is
visible both in the divergence statistics and in the noise feed-through
cost of the consistent filter. The natural next step is control that
consumes the covariance rather than ignoring it: the covariance-aware LQR
variant implemented here (weight inflation, in the spirit of cautious
control) is the minimal version, and covariance steering
\cite{ridderhof2021covariance} and reachable-set-constrained impulsive
planning \cite{chernick2021reachable,hunter2025cislunar} are the mature
formulations. The framework's configured certainty-equivalence stress test
(covariance-aware LQR at the loose-navigation corner,
\texttt{config/ablation.yaml}) is deferred to follow-on work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The truth is $J_2$--$J_4$ zonal-only by design; differential drag and
solar radiation pressure --- significant for low-altitude or
high-area-ratio formations \cite{gaias2015drag,guffanti2019srp} --- are
excluded so that model error is attributable, and including them would
enlarge the secular residual the consistency analysis identifies rather
than remove it. Actuator limits are applied asymmetrically: the LQR
acceleration is
magnitude-limited at $2\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, whereas the MPC and
impulsive schemes are run without a delta-V bound, so their cost figures
are lower bounds with respect to a thrust-limited vehicle and the
three-way cost comparison should be read with that in mind. The campaign
also fixes a near-circular chief at $i = 98^\circ$, a single 1-km formation
scale, and one tuning per controller; the perfect-state sweeps vary
tightness but the
estimation-in-the-loop grid does not. Measurement models are white and
unbiased --- no multipath, integer-ambiguity, or attitude-coupling errors
--- so the reliability map is, if anything, optimistic. The main results
use the EKF; the implemented UKF ablation and the covariance-aware LQR
stress test are configured but not part of the reported campaign. Finally,
the analytic models are first-order in $J_2$; at 1-km scale the
linearization error is subdominant to the secular $J_2$ residual, but both
grow with formation size.
