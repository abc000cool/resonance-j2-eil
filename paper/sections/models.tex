\section{Relative-motion models}\label{sec:models}

This section summarizes the four analytic relative-motion models carried by
the framework in increasing order of $J_2$ fidelity, and the formation
geometry families swept in the campaign. Detailed state definitions and the
implementation-level symbol table are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:stm};
every model is verified in the accompanying test suite against both its
defining differential equations and the numerical truth of
Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{LVLH frame and Clohessy--Wiltshire baseline}

The local-vertical/local-horizontal (LVLH) frame is centered on the chief
with $x$ radial, $z$ along the orbit normal, and $y$ completing the triad
(along-track for a circular orbit). For an unperturbed circular chief with
mean motion $n$, the linearized relative dynamics are the
Clohessy--Wiltshire (CW) equations \cite{clohessy1960terminal}, whose
closed-form STM is the classical rendezvous baseline (the eccentric
generalization is due to Tschauner and Hempel
\cite{tschauner1965rendezvous}). CW carries no $J_2$ physics at all; it is
retained as the zero-fidelity control case.

\subsection{$J_2$ secular dynamics and differential drift}

To first order, $J_2$ produces secular rates in the right ascension of the
ascending node, argument of perigee, and mean anomaly
\cite{brouwer1959solution,vallado2013fundamentals}. Two spacecraft with
slightly different orbital elements experience slightly different rates,
and the resulting \emph{differential} drift --- dominated by the
along-track runaway from differential nodal and anomalistic rates --- is
the operational problem formation-keeping must solve. Mean and osculating
elements are related by the first-order Brouwer--Lyddane transformation
\cite{brouwer1959solution,lyddane1963small}; all analytic models below
propagate mean elements, and the framework converts to and from osculating
states at the truth interface. For particular element combinations the
differential secular rates cancel, giving the $J_2$-invariant conditions of
Schaub and Alfriend \cite{schaub2001j2invariant}; general formations do not
satisfy them, which is why active control is required.

\subsection{Schweighart--Sedwick LTI plant}

Schweighart and Sedwick \cite{schweighart2002highfidelity,
schweighart2001thesis} modify the CW system with constant coefficients
that capture the orbit-averaged effect of $J_2$ on a circular reference
orbit, yielding a linear time-invariant (LTI) plant with an analytically
available STM and a small cross-track forcing term. Its LTI structure makes
it the natural plant for the steady-state LQR of
Section~\ref{sec:controllers}. The implementation reproduces the published
coefficient set and is verified against direct integration of the
Schweighart--Sedwick ODEs.

\subsection{Gim--Alfriend geometric STM}

The Gim--Alfriend STM \cite{gim2003state,gim2005equinoctial,
alfriend2010formation} propagates the relative state to first order in
$J_2$ including eccentricity, through the geometric composition
$\Phi(t,t_0) = A(t)\,\Sigma(t,t_0)\,B(t_0)$, where $A$ and $B$ map between
curvilinear relative coordinates and differential elements and $\Sigma$
propagates the differential elements. Rather than transcribing the lengthy
closed-form entries, the implementation constructs $A$, $\Sigma$, and $B$
by numerical linearization of the exact nonlinear maps about the chief ---
the same construction to first order, with the transcription risk traded
for two extra digits of linearization error, verified to be equivalent to
the closed form within first-order $J_2$ accuracy.

\subsection{Quasi-nonsingular ROE and the KGD STM}

The quasi-nonsingular relative orbital elements (ROE)
\cite{damico2010thesis,damico2006proximity,sullivan2017survey} express the
deputy state as scaled element differences --- relative semi-major axis,
relative mean longitude, and the relative eccentricity and inclination
vectors --- in which $J_2$ acts almost linearly and near-secularly.
Koenig, Guffanti, and D'Amico \cite{koenig2017stm} derived closed-form
$J_2$ STMs in these coordinates valid for arbitrary eccentricity (their
Eq.~(A6) for the quasi-nonsingular set); this ``KGD'' STM is the
highest-fidelity analytic model carried here, and the plant used by the MPC
controller. Extensions incorporating differential drag and solar radiation
pressure exist \cite{gaias2015drag,guffanti2019srp} but are outside the
$J_2$-isolation scope of this study. The linear ROE$\to$LVLH map and its
Jacobian, needed to fuse LVLH-frame measurements into ROE-native filters,
follow D'Amico \cite{damico2010thesis}.

\subsection{Formation geometry families}

The campaign sweeps three (screening: four) standard 1-km-class geometry
families (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}): pure along-track separation, the
passively safe eccentricity/inclination-vector-separated geometry of
D'Amico and Montenbruck \cite{damico2006proximity} (``E/I-safe''), a
projected circular orbit (PCO), and, in the screening tier only, a general
elliptical relative orbit (GERO); the screening data showed GERO's
delta-V behavior to be strongly correlated with E/I-safe
($\rho = 0.88$ across the grid), and it was pruned from the full tier.

\subsection{Model validation against the numerical truth}

Figure~\ref{fig:validation} shows open-loop prediction error of each
analytic model against the $J_2$--$J_4$ numerical truth over 30 days for a
1-km E/I-safe formation. The ordering follows fidelity: CW and
Schweighart--Sedwick accumulate kilometer-class error over the month, while the first-order-$J_2$ element-space models
remain far below --- Gim--Alfriend near $100$~m and KGD at $17$--$26$~m.
Two properties of this figure matter later: the errors of the element-space
models are \emph{secular}, not random, growing monotonically with time; and
a two-order-of-magnitude gap separates the best and worst models. Both
facts return with consequences in the filter-consistency analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:results}.
